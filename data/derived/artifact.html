Beyond the R1s

R1 institutions run of ACCESS computing. R2s, Research Colleges & Universities (RCUs) and other colleges run while holding of current projects. About half of that gap tracks research spending. Most of the rest is Maximize-scale computing, where non-R1 institutions are far below their research share. Reach and thin campus engagement are the other clear gaps.

Are awards distributed fairly? Measured against research spending, mostly yes for Explore, Discover and Accelerate: R2s and RCUs hold of recent awards against of research spending. Not for Maximize, where they hold of projects, and non-R1 academic PIs as a whole run of Maximize usage. This data cannot say whether that reflects need or access.

Is there a recruitment gap? Yes. Outside medical schools, R2s and RCUs have not used ACCESS since 2022.

Do experience and allocation type explain lower use? Partly. The typical recent non-R1 project has used less of its award than the typical R1 project ( against ). PI experience and allocation type account for about a third of that difference, and the rest is within the noise.

08 · What would move it

Where to act, and what to find out.

Large allocations

Find out why non-R1 Maximize awards are rare.

This is the largest piece of the gap and the least understood. Allocations request and review data (who applies for Maximize, and with what outcome) would show whether it is demand, proposal capacity or review. Helping sustained Explore and Discover users move up is one plausible route.

Reach

Recruit at research-active R2s and RCUs.

Start with the institutions in unreached_institutions.csv, largest research programs first, through research offices.

Depth

Add a second PI on thin campuses.

of active colleges rely on one PI. A second PI, through Campus Champions or a shared allocation, should help keep the campus active when the first moves on.

Starting

Check in with new PIs early.

New non-R1 PIs drop off sooner. An early check-in and help running a first job are, in our judgment, cheap to try. The evidence here is suggestive rather than firm.

Notes and method

  1. Usage. Batch HPC from the XDMoD Jobs realm (ACCESS Credit Equivalents, ACEs) plus Jetstream2 from the Cloud realm, converted to SUs at Jetstream2's documented rates (1 SU per core-hour for CPU, 2 for large-memory and the GPU flavors in use) and to credits at the Allocations catalog's one credit per SU. Window to . Usage is attributed to the PI's institution, so a non-R1 collaborator on an R1 project counts as R1. Credits exchanged into storage, OSG, TAMU Launch or CloudBank produce no measured usage.
  2. Research spending. Higher-education R&D expenditure (HERD), averaged FY21–23, from the Carnegie 2025 file. It is published only for R1, R2 and RCU institutions, so non-designated colleges are left out of these comparisons. Proportionality to research money is not the same as equity.
  3. Health-science institutions. Standalone medical and health-science schools (the 2021 Carnegie special-focus classes, e.g. Icahn, the Medical College of Wisconsin, OHSU) carry large biomedical budgets and little computing demand, while integrated R1s carry the same spending invisibly inside the parent. They are left out of the research-spending comparisons. Counting them, R2s look short on awards ( of recent awards against of spending).
  4. Awards. The Allocations feed lists current projects. Its ACCESS Credits line is the balance left after exchanges, not the award, so awards are rebuilt by converting exchanged resource units back to credits with the catalog's conversion factors. Those are today's factors, which reproduce standard award sizes only for recent projects, so award shares use the projects begun since 2025 whose resources all convert. Older, multi-year Maximize awards accumulate several years of resource lines and cannot be compared fairly, so the Maximize comparison uses project counts and usage instead.
  5. Never used. An awarded institution with no XDMoD job or cloud record since September 2022. Recent awards are included, so some have simply not started, and only the R1 list has been checked by hand for name mismatches.
  6. Use of awards. Explore, Discover and Accelerate projects with rebuilt awards, begun in the twelve months before the window end and at least three months old ( non-R1, R1), joined to their own usage by charge number. Older projects still in the feed are the ones that were extended, so they are left out. Share used = batch HPC plus cloud usage since the project began, divided by the credits it could use (exchanged into HPC or Jetstream2, plus not yet exchanged), prorated over a twelve-month term. Experienced = the PI's name had usage as PI in XDMoD before the project began (name match, so common names can collide).
  7. Uncertainty. 95% intervals from 2,000 bootstrap resamples. Median gap to . After adjusting for experience and type, to . Projects with no usage yet: against (difference to points). Among projects with no Jetstream2 award the medians are and . Pooled usage over pooled credits is closer ( against ) because usage is concentrated in large projects.
  8. Allocation types. Usage share is batch HPC over the whole window ( since 2025); award share is projects begun since 2025. Both are R1 plus non-R1 academic only.
  9. PI persistence. Batch HPC only. Continuing = usage 12 or more months after the PI's first ACCESS-era month, counted for PIs whose first month is at least a year before the window end. New here = no usage as PI from 2017 until ACCESS began. The RCU and college groups are small ( and new PIs).
  10. Trends and context. Batch HPC only; the pipeline pulls monthly cloud usage by allocation, not by institution. Institution counts are XDMoD labels, and a few institutions appear under more than one label.

Tiers are Carnegie 2025 Research Activity Designations matched by normalized name, with manual overrides in data/overrides.json; units of R1s (NCSA, PSC, SDSC, TACC, R1 medical schools) are folded into R1. HBCU and tribal-college flags are IPEDS HD2023. The full method and every judgment call are in the repository README.
