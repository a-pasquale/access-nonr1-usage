ACCESS-CI · PI-institution usage analysis · –

Beyond the R1s

Non-R1 academic institutions hold of current ACCESS projects and generate of the compute. Awards follow research spending once standalone medical schools are set aside, and per awarded credit, non-R1 Discover and Accelerate projects show usage similar to R1 ones. The gap sits elsewhere: fewer non-R1 institutions reach ACCESS at all, non-R1 campuses typically depend on one or two PIs, Explore projects convert less, and R1s hold nearly all of the large Maximize-scale work. This page measures each, and what would move it.

Is the award share itself a gap?

One check is proportionality to academic research spending (HERD, averaged FY21–23, from the Carnegie file). That tests fit to research money, not equity, and it leaves out non-designated colleges, which have no published spending. Standalone health-science institutions need separate treatment. Schools like Icahn, the Medical College of Wisconsin and OHSU carry large biomedical budgets and little HPC demand, while integrated R1s carry the same kind of spending invisibly inside the parent. Counting them, R2s look short, with of awarded credits against of spending. Without them, awards follow spending closely at every tier, as the table shows. Usage does not: R1s run of it.

Weighted by research spending the gap is smaller: reached institutions hold of R2 spending and of RCU spending, and much of the R2 remainder is at medical schools. At the one-year mark, PIs continue at similar rates

Following individual PIs instead of institutions changes the picture. A PI counts as continuing if they still have usage 12 or more months after their first job in the ACCESS era. of R1 PIs continue, and of non-R1 academic PIs. Two qualifications matter. New non-R1 PIs drop off sooner: are still active six to eleven months in, against at R1s. And the non-R1 figure is lifted by its larger share of experienced PIs: at the R1 mix of new and experienced PIs it would be . Among new PIs, those at R2s continue of the time, but at RCUs and at non-designated colleges, against at R1s. The RCU and college groups are small ( and PIs), so treat those two figures as indicative.

Being new matters most, and Explore matters too. New PIs who ever used Explore continue of the time at R1s and at non-R1s, against and for new PIs who never did. XDMoD does not record which allocation came first, so "ever used Explore" is a proxy.

New = no usage as PI in XDMoD from 2017 until ACCESS began. Continuing is counted only for PIs whose first ACCESS-era month is at least 12 months before the window end. A PI who changed institutions counts as new.

Experience does not explain the usage gap. Non-R1 campuses have a larger share of experienced PIs than R1s ( against ), and within each group the median non-R1 PI uses less: ACEs against among new PIs, and against among experienced ones.

The institution gap is consistent with how thin non-R1 engagement is. The median R1 has active PIs. The median RCU has and the median non-designated college has , and of those colleges depend on a single PI. When one PI at an R1 stops, the institution stays active. When the only PI at a college stops, the college drops out.

Support for new Explore PIs belongs in the program as a whole, because the drop-off appears at R1s and non-R1s alike. What is specific to non-R1s is earlier drop-off among new PIs, weaker continuation at RCUs and colleges, and depth: getting a second and third PI active on a campus so engagement survives one person moving on.

05 · How they compute

Conventional HPC at lower volume — not a different kind of user.

Measured by volume, non-R1 computing looks like R1 computing: most of their ACEs come from jobs of 9–256 cores, and most from jobs running 18 hours or more. By job count they run more small, short jobs than R1s. They lean toward the general-purpose CPU systems (Expanse, Bridges-2 RM) and use GPUs at about the same rate as R1s ( of usage against ), with less on the newest AI systems such as DeltaAI. Gateways carry almost none of their usage. ChemCompute at Sonoma State accounts for of non-R1 gateway jobs but only of gateway ACEs.

07 · What would move it

Four interventions the data points at.

Reach

Recruit at research-active R2s and RCUs.

R2s and RCUs have no ACCESS usage since 2022. Setting aside medical schools, that leaves R2s and RCUs. unreached_institutions.csv ranks them by research spending, so research-office outreach can start with the largest programs. Our judgment is that institutions with active research programs are the easiest to bring in.

Activation

Call the awarded-never-used list.

Up to non-R1 academic institutions hold a current award and have no recorded usage since September 2022. The list has not been checked for name variants and includes recent awards, so it needs a pass by hand first. Those that remain have already applied and been awarded. A first-job concierge (login, environment, one submitted batch script) is, in our judgment, among the cheapest ways to add usage.

Persistence

Support new Explore PIs, and add a second PI on thin campuses.

New PIs who use Explore continue past a year about half the time at R1s and non-R1s alike, so check-ins at months 3 and 9 of an Explore award belong in the program as a whole. New non-R1 PIs drop off sooner, which argues for an early check-in, before month six. For non-R1s the specific problem is depth: of active non-designated colleges rely on one PI. Introducing a second PI on those campuses, through Campus Champions or a shared allocation, should help keep the institution active when the first one moves on.

Growth

Nudge sustained Explore users into Discover.

Per awarded credit, non-R1 Discover and Accelerate projects show usage similar to R1 ones, so moving sustained Explore PIs up is a plausible route to volume. PIs with usage in six of the last twelve months are the graduation candidates. A templated Discover request and a resource-fit conversation (the most common non-R1 resources are Expanse and Bridges-2 RM) could turn participation into volume.

Method and caveats

Usage is from the ACCESS XDMoD Jobs realm, to , grouped by PI institution ( institutions, B ACEs, reconciling exactly to XDMoD's unfiltered total). Institutions were matched to the Carnegie 2025 Research Activity Designations public data file by normalized name, with manual overrides (data/overrides.json) for campus-system naming (e.g. "University of Michigan" → Ann Arbor); every R1 match was hand-checked. Units of R1s (NCSA, PSC, SDSC, TACC, medical schools, observatories) were folded into R1. Institutions not in the Carnegie research file were classed as other academic, non-academic (labs, industry, hospitals, government, nonprofits) or foreign/unknown by name. Non-academic PIs (2.4% of usage) are shown for completeness but excluded from "non-R1 academic".

Awarded projects are the current projects in the ACCESS Allocations public feed at the time of the pull, with awards rebuilt by converting each project's exchanged resource units back to ACCESS Credits using the conversion factors in the Allocations resource catalog. The feed's own ACCESS Credits line is the balance left after exchanges, not the award. Today's factors reproduce standard award sizes only for recent projects, so credit comparisons use projects begun in 2025 or later. Standalone health-science institutions are the 2021 Carnegie special-focus medical, research-institution and health-professions classes. PI-level figures come from a separate XDMoD pull by PI, keyed by hashed PI labels. "Never used" means the institution has no XDMoD job record at all in the four-year window; the R1 figure is mostly name variants and should be read as noise. HBCU and tribal-college flags are from IPEDS HD2023 via UNITID; IPEDS carries no HSI flag. Retention counts an institution as active in a quarter if any job was charged; partial quarters are excluded from quarterly charts. Program years run September–August.
